## Matrix-Matched Correction for Portable Turbidity and Nitrate Testing in High-Hardness Banat Wells

**Pair:** Goran Radulović (R0679, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry) with Kristal Voda preduzetnik (C015), challenge C015-CH2
**Form:** Joint research project, from the researcher's consent: "joint project applications related to portable water quality monitoring or aquifer characterisation"
**Critic:** approved after 1 draft(s). Screening score 2.94 / 3.

### The problem
Kristal Voda preduzetnik’s portable turbidity and nitrate test kits give inconsistent readings in hard water above 450 mg/L CaCO3, with field samples from Banat wells sometimes reaching 550 mg/L CaCO3. The current distilled-water calibration does not match the sample matrix, and the observed error is around 20–30% versus reference lab results. The practical problem is to develop a handheld, field-usable correction or calibration approach that works for Kristal Voda’s technicians without requiring routine external laboratory confirmation.

### Research questions
1. Can a matrix-matched calibration standard for waters above 450 mg/L CaCO3 reduce turbidity and nitrate error to within ±10% of reference laboratory values in Banat well samples?
2. Which field-usable correction approach performs better for the current kits: a single correction factor, a hardness-stratified correction, or a matrix-matched standard addition protocol?
3. Does the current reagent/sensor chemistry remain fit for purpose at 450 to 550 mg/L CaCO3, or do the observed biases require a change to the kit chemistry or operating range?

## Industrial PhD on feedstock-specification and pre-processing control for Suncokret Eko d.o.o.'s pellet press line

**Pair:** Veljko Savić (R0135, Department of Production Engineering) with Suncokret Eko d.o.o. (C007), challenge C007-CH3
**Form:** Industrial PhD, from the researcher's consent: "industrial PhD supervision for candidates from biomass processing or agricultural machinery companies"
**Critic:** approved after 2 draft(s). Screening score 2.99 / 3.

### The problem
Suncokret Eko d.o.o. loses about 15,000 EUR per pellet-line breakdown, and the line goes down roughly once a month because sunflower husk feedstock arrives with moisture content between 8% and 22% and particle sizes ranging from fine powder to chunks over 15 mm. The company needs to define the acceptable feedstock range for its current press line, determine which incoming variance can be tolerated, and test whether pre-processing with a secondary hammer mill and a conditioning mixer would keep the material inside that range and reduce failures.

### Research questions
1. What moisture and particle-size window keeps Suncokret Eko d.o.o.'s current pellet press line operating without clogging or bearing failures, and how wide is the tolerated variance within that window?
2. How much do moisture correction and particle-size conditioning, separately and in combination, reduce the monthly breakdown rate and the associated downtime cost under Suncokret Eko d.o.o.'s current operating conditions?
3. Can an intake inspection rule combining the existing in-line NIR moisture sensor with a practical particle-size check classify incoming lots well enough to prevent out-of-spec material from reaching the press?

### Plan
| Period | Work | Result | Pass if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Year 1 | Map the current failure history against incoming moisture and particle-size measurements, define measurable degradation indicators for clogging and bearing stress, and calibrate a lot-level intake classification rule using Suncokret Eko d.o.o.'s existing line data and production logs. The work will also establish the baseline for downtime cost under the current process. | A validated baseline model linking feedstock variability to monthly press breakdowns, plus an initial moisture and particle-size acceptance envelope for the current line. | At least 12 months of historical or reconstructed operating records are analysed; the baseline model explains the direction of failure risk for both moisture and particle-size effects; and the first draft of the acceptance envelope covers the full observed incoming range from 8% to 22% moisture and from fine powder to particles over 15 mm. |
| Year 2 | Run controlled production trials on the current line to test moisture adjustment and particle-size reduction, including the proposed secondary hammer mill and conditioning mixer only if they are available for the trial. Compare untreated, moisture-corrected, particle-size-corrected, and combined-preprocessing lots, and check whether the existing NIR sensor plus a particle-size check can reliably separate acceptable from unacceptable lots. Any step involving altered operating settings will remain off the live installation until Suncokret Eko d.o.o.'s own safety review approves it. | An experimentally tested pre-processing specification showing which combinations of moisture correction and particle-size conditioning keep the line inside the acceptable operating window. | At least three trial conditions are completed with repeated lots; the best condition reduces breakdown indicators by at least 50% versus the baseline lots; and the intake rule correctly classifies at least 80% of trial lots as acceptable or unacceptable relative to the experimentally observed operating window. |
| Year 3 | Convert the trial results into an operating decision rule for procurement, intake inspection, and pre-processing, then verify transfer to Suncokret Eko d.o.o.'s own equipment and workflow. Final validation will use company staff, the company's line, and its own feedstock stream to confirm whether the recommended specification holds under routine production. | A company-ready feedstock specification, inspection rule, and pre-processing recommendation that can be used to reduce monthly pellet-line failures and their cost. | The final operating rule is tested on at least one routine production cycle on Suncokret Eko d.o.o.'s own line; the verified result shows fewer out-of-spec lots reaching the press than under the current intake practice; and the company can state which feedstock range should be rejected, conditioned, or accepted. |

3. How well do protocol-aware versus protocol-agnostic features transfer from ArmaturaSec’s archive to the company’s hardware bench and then to a live customer site after an explicit transfer check?
