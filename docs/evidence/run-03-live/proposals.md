## Condition window and pre-conditioning strategy for reliable sunflower-husk pelletising at Suncokret Eko d.o.o.

**Pair:** Veljko Savić (R0135, Department of Production Engineering) with Suncokret Eko d.o.o. (C007), challenge C007-CH3
**Critic:** approved after 1 draft(s). Screening score 2.99 / 3.

### The problem
Suncokret Eko d.o.o. runs a pellet press line that breaks down about once a month, with each incident costing roughly 15,000 EUR in lost production, emergency maintenance, and contract penalties. The dominant cause is variable sunflower husk feedstock from different suppliers: moisture ranges from 8% to 22%, particle size ranges from fine powder to chunks above 15 mm, the press clogs when moisture exceeds 14%, and uneven compression and bearing failures occur when the fraction of fines/coarse pieces is too high. The company currently samples at intake, rejects visibly bad loads, and uses an in-line NIR moisture sensor that is unreliable below 10% and above 18%, but it has no automated particle-size check. It is considering a secondary hammer mill and a conditioning mixer, yet does not know whether these steps would stabilize the incoming material enough to prevent failures. The industrial PhD would define the acceptable feedstock window and quantify which pre-processing steps are sufficient to keep the press inside that window.

### Research questions
1. What combined ranges of moisture content and particle-size distribution can the current sunflower-husk pellet press tolerate before clogging or bearing-failure risk rises sharply?
2. Which pre-conditioning option—secondary hammer milling, conditioning mixing, or their combination—most effectively brings supplier material into the acceptable operating window while minimizing added energy, handling time, and cost?
3. Can a practical intake-control rule be built from existing moisture measurements, simple particle-size sampling, and line operating data to predict when a batch should be redirected, pre-conditioned, or rejected?

### Plan
| Year | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Map historical breakdowns against recorded intake moisture, particle size, supplier, and operating conditions; set up a simple feedstock sampling protocol and collect controlled batch trials on the plant line and pilot press with the company staff. | A quantified failure-risk map and an initial acceptable feedstock window for moisture and particle size. |
| 2 | Run designed experiments comparing no pre-treatment, secondary hammer milling, conditioning mixing, and combined pre-treatment under representative supplier material; measure pellet quality, throughput, energy use, and press stability. | A validated pre-conditioning specification with expected reduction in downtime and operating cost. |
| 3 | Develop and test a decision rule for intake and pre-processing using moisture and particle-size inputs; validate it over a full production season and translate results into an operating procedure for procurement and production staff. | A deployable intake-control guideline and maintenance/operation protocol that reduces monthly breakdowns. |

### What each side brings
- **Company:** Suncokret Eko d.o.o. brings the real production line, historical downtime and intake records, supplier variation across fourteen farms, staff for trials, and the practical constraints of a 31-person biomass business operating at Novi Sad with limited in-house research capacity. The company can provide access to drying, hammer-mill, screening, and pelletising operations, plus its existing NIR moisture data and process logs.
- **Faculty:** R0135 brings expertise in biomass densification reliability, condition-based maintenance, stochastic failure modelling, and feedstock-variability effects on pellet-mill performance. Veljko Savić and his group can design the experiments, analyse failure data, build the statistical decision model, and supervise the use of the instrumented pilot pellet press and the Laboratory for Quality and Reliability at FTN. Supervisors: academic supervisor Veljko Savić (R0135), company supervisor Dragan Ostojić (process lead at Suncokret Eko d.o.o.).

### Why this pair
This pair fits because the company has a sharply defined, recurring production loss caused by feedstock variability, while the researcher has directly relevant expertise in biomass densification reliability, pellet-mill failure modelling, vibration and fault detection, and maintenance decision support. The company already operates the exact process class under study and has enough data and trial capacity to support a rigorous industrial PhD, while the researcher has prior consulting and project experience on biomass densification equipment and on-site instrumentation with industry partners.

### First step
Within the next month, the partners should agree on a one-page trial protocol: which breakdown and intake variables will be logged, how moisture and particle size will be sampled at each delivery for four weeks, which lots will be kept untreated versus pre-conditioned in controlled trials, and who will sign off on data sharing and sample handling.

## Matrix-robust field calibration for portable turbidity and nitrate testing in hard Banat groundwater

**Pair:** Goran Radulović (R0679, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry) with Kristal Voda preduzetnik (C015), challenge C015-CH2
**Critic:** approved after 1 draft(s). Screening score 2.94 / 3.

### The problem
Kristal Voda’s portable turbidity and nitrate kits were specified for waters up to about 300 mg/L CaCO₃ hardness, but several Banat well sources now exceed 450 mg/L CaCO₃ and can reach 550 mg/L. In those samples, turbidity reads 20–30% higher than the reference lab and nitrate can read low by a similar margin. The company cannot send every sample to an external lab and needs a fast, handheld, technician-friendly fix that works on site, not a laboratory method. The central problem is therefore to determine whether matrix-matched calibration, a practical correction factor, or a small change to the kit chemistry can deliver reliable field results in hard groundwater without adding complexity for non-chemist technicians.

### Research questions
1. Can a matrix-matched calibration standard prepared for 450–550 mg/L CaCO₃ groundwater reduce turbidity and nitrate bias to within ±10% of reference-lab values across Banat well samples?
2. Which correction strategy is most robust in field use: a single hardness-based correction factor, a two-point matrix calibration, or a small kit-chemistry adjustment that technicians can apply with handheld equipment?
3. What minimum field protocol can Kristal Voda technicians follow so that repeatability stays acceptable in samples above 450 mg/L CaCO₃ hardness while preserving the speed of on-site testing?

### Plan
| Year | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Map the interference: collect paired field and reference-lab measurements from Banat wells spanning roughly 300 to 550 mg/L CaCO₃ hardness; test current kits against hardness, conductivity, and sample matrix composition; screen candidate matrix standards and simple correction models. | A quantified interference model and an initial field protocol showing where the present kits fail and which calibration approach is most promising. |
| 2 | Develop and validate the best mitigation route: prepare matrix-matched standards, test correction factors, and if needed adjust reagent concentration or sample-prep steps; run side-by-side field trials with Kristal Voda technicians on real client sites. | A validated technician-friendly method for hard water, with documented accuracy and repeatability targets for turbidity and nitrate. |
| 3 | Turn the method into a deployable package: finalize a one-page SOP, calibration kit insert, training checklist, and acceptance limits; assess robustness across season, well source, and operator. | A ready-to-use field package that Kristal Voda can supply with its kits and maintain in routine service visits. |

### What each side brings
- **Company:** Kristal Voda brings access to the exact problem setting: Banat well sources above 450 mg/L CaCO₃, routine field technicians, portable turbidity and nitrate kits, and the ability to run repeated on-site trials during normal service visits. Supervisor on company side: Miroslav Đurić.
- **Faculty:** The Faculty of Technology team brings expertise in analytical chemistry, environmental chemistry, water-quality monitoring, field analysis, portable spectrophotometry, and matrix-interference correction in high-ionic-strength groundwater. Supervisor on faculty side: Goran Radulović.

### Why this pair
This pair is unusually well matched because the company’s problem is exactly a field-matrix interference problem, and the researcher has a record of solving interference suppression in portable colorimetric methods for groundwater. Kristal Voda has the real hard-water samples, field access, and operational constraints; the university group has the instrumentation and methods expertise needed to turn that problem into a robust, low-cost correction that technicians can use immediately.

### First step
Next month, the partners should select 10–15 representative Banat wells, collect duplicate samples alongside current kit readings and reference-lab results, and agree on a test matrix of hardness levels, calibration options, and success criteria for turbidity and nitrate bias.

## Site-Specific Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Modbus and BACnet Traffic on Embedded OT Appliances

**Pair:** Krisztina Kiss (R1008, Department of Computing and Control Engineering) with ArmaturaSec d.o.o. (C001), challenge C001-CH1
**Critic:** approved after 1 draft(s). Screening score 2.97 / 3.

### The problem
ArmaturaSec’s ProtoSense module must flag unusual behaviour on Modbus and BACnet networks without any labelled attack traffic. The company has several terabytes of normal packet captures from consenting clients, but no confirmed malicious examples, and a prior supervised approach trained on public ICS datasets produced too many false positives in real deployments, causing customers to disable alerts. The target is a method that learns a site-specific baseline from unlabeled traffic, detects drift from that baseline, and can run on-device in the company’s embedded Linux appliance with limited engineering effort.

### Research questions
1. Can an unsupervised or one-class model trained only on site-specific Modbus and BACnet traffic detect abnormal behaviour with materially lower false-positive rates than the current supervised baseline when evaluated on archived captures and controlled testbed replay?
2. Which protocol-aware features and baseline windows best balance detection sensitivity and explainability for plant operators, while remaining lightweight enough for inference on ArmaturaSec’s on-device hardware?
3. How stable is the learned baseline over weeks or months of normal operation, and what online adaptation strategy prevents alert fatigue without hiding genuine drift?

### Plan
| Year | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Collect and curate representative Modbus/BACnet traffic from ArmaturaSec archives; define protocol-aware features; build an offline baseline model and evaluation protocol using the company’s traffic and the university ICS testbed. | A validated dataset schema, a first anomaly detector, and measurable offline benchmarks on false positives and detection latency. |
| 2 | Integrate the best model into a prototype version of ProtoSense; optimize inference for the embedded Linux environment; test on hardware-in-the-loop benches with PLC simulators and replayed traffic. | An on-device prototype with documented resource use, explainable alert outputs, and performance results on representative OT scenarios. |
| 3 | Study baseline drift and online adaptation across multiple sites; refine alert suppression and calibration rules with engineer and operator feedback; prepare deployment guidance for ArmaturaSec support staff. | A production-ready detection workflow, deployment guide, and joint publication on site-specific unsupervised OT anomaly detection. |

### What each side brings
- **Company:** ArmaturaSec brings the practical problem, several terabytes of normal Modbus and BACnet captures, access to ProtoSense firmware and embedded hardware under NDA, and one-to-two engineers part-time for integration and validation. The company can also test the method on its hardware bench and in customer-like OT environments.
- **Faculty:** Krisztina Kiss brings expertise in industrial cybersecurity, ICS traffic analysis, unsupervised anomaly detection, one-class classification, and long-term measurement on industrial control networks. Her group also brings an ICS testbed with Siemens-compatible PLCs, Modbus/TCP traffic generators, packet capture infrastructure, and experience validating methods on operational infrastructure.

### Why this pair
This pair matches the problem unusually well: ArmaturaSec needs a no-label, site-specific detector for Modbus/BACnet that must run on-device, and Krisztina Kiss’s group has directly relevant research on statistical baseline modelling, lightweight anomaly detection for ICS traffic, and protocol-aware traffic analysis. ArmaturaSec provides real OT traffic and deployment constraints; the university provides the method development and test environment needed to turn those constraints into a robust detector.
