## Matrix-matched correction for portable turbidity and nitrate kits in hard Banat groundwater

**Pair:** Goran Radulović (R0679, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry) with Kristal Voda preduzetnik (C015), challenge C015-CH2
**Critic:** approved after 2 draft(s). Screening score 2.94 / 3.

### The problem
Kristal Voda’s portable turbidity and nitrate kits were specified for water up to about 300 mg/L CaCO3 hardness, but several Banat well sources they now service are at 450–550 mg/L CaCO3. In those field samples, turbidity readings are 20–30% higher than a reference lab and nitrate readings can be similarly low. The company currently calibrates with distilled water or Zrenjanin tap water, neither of which matches the sample matrix. Because Kristal Voda needs fast on-site results during a visit and cannot afford to send every sample to an external lab, the practical question is whether a simple matrix-matched calibration standard, a correction factor, or a kit chemistry adjustment can make the handheld workflow reliable for hard groundwater.

### Research questions
1. Can a matrix-matched calibration set built from representative Banat groundwater levels of hardness, alkalinity, calcium, and magnesium reduce turbidity and nitrate bias from 20–30% to within an agreed field tolerance relative to laboratory reference results?
2. Which matrix variables most strongly explain the error in the portable kits at 450–550 mg/L CaCO3 hardness: hardness itself, alkalinity, ionic strength, or a combination, and can a single correction model be validated for routine use by technicians?
3. Can the corrected procedure be simplified into a technician-friendly field protocol that works with handheld equipment and does not require external laboratory confirmation for routine on-site visits?

### Plan
| Year | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Map the failure mode on a shared set of Banat well samples and reference waters; measure turbidity and nitrate errors against lab methods under controlled hardness/alkalinity conditions; build a small calibration dataset and test matrix-matched standards versus simple correction factors. | A quantified error model for the current kits and a first validated correction approach for hard water samples. |
| 2 | Optimize the chemistry and workflow of the existing kits using the error model; compare revised reagent handling, dilution, and matrix-matched calibration in field trials with Kristal Voda technicians on real client wells. | A field-usable protocol that reduces bias and variability under 450–550 mg/L CaCO3 conditions. |
| 3 | Stress-test the selected solution across seasons, wells, and technicians; finalize a robust operating procedure, training sheet, and acceptance criteria; prepare publication and company implementation package. | A deployable method package for routine use plus a joint scientific output on matrix effects in portable water testing. |

### What each side brings
- **Company:** Kristal Voda brings access to the exact problem setting: Banat well sources with hardness around 450–550 mg/L CaCO3, the current portable turbidity and nitrate kits, field technicians, and routine client visits where the method can be tested under real operating conditions. The company also brings practical constraints that define the target solution: handheld equipment, fast on-site results, and a small team with no dedicated R&D staff.
- **Faculty:** The Faculty of Technology brings Goran Radulović’s expertise in analytical chemistry, environmental chemistry, water quality monitoring, field analysis, portable spectrophotometry, and matrix-interference correction, plus a small field-analytical laboratory with portable spectrophotometers, ion chromatography, ICP-OES, and titration equipment. His group can design the calibration study, identify the matrix drivers, and validate a correction strategy against reference measurements.

### Why this pair
This pair is a strong fit because the company’s problem is exactly the kind of matrix effect challenge addressed by Radulović’s work: portable field methods in difficult waters with high ionic strength, alkalinity, and divalent cation interference. Kristal Voda already serves the affected Banat wells and can provide repeated access to real samples and field use, while the university can supply the measurement rigor and interference expertise needed to turn a workaround into a reliable operating procedure.

### First step
Next month, the partners should collect 15–20 paired samples from Banat wells spanning roughly 300 to 550 mg/L CaCO3 hardness and measure each one with the current portable kits and the faculty reference workflow, so they can decide whether the best route is matrix-matched calibration, a correction factor, or a kit modification.

## Stabilising sunflower husk pelletising at Suncokret Eko through feedstock acceptance limits and pre-processing control

**Pair:** Veljko Savić (R0135, Department of Production Engineering) with Suncokret Eko d.o.o. (C007), challenge C007-CH3
**Critic:** approved after 2 draft(s). Screening score 2.99 / 3.

### The problem
Suncokret Eko d.o.o. (Novi Sad, 31 employees) loses about one pellet-line breakdown per month because sunflower husk deliveries vary too much in moisture and particle size. Moisture can range from 8% to 22%, with die clogging above 14%, while mixed fines and chunks above 15 mm create uneven compression and bearing failures. Each incident costs about 15,000 EUR in lost production, emergency maintenance, and contract penalties. The company currently relies on manual intake checks and an in-line NIR sensor that is weak below 10% and above 18% moisture, and it has no automated particle-size check. The open question is what feedstock variance is actually acceptable for the press, and whether a secondary hammer mill and/or conditioning mixer can reliably bring current supplier material inside that range.

### Research questions
1. What incoming sunflower husk moisture and particle-size windows minimise clogging, bearing failures, and unplanned stoppages on the current pellet press, using Suncokret Eko’s supplier and downtime data?
2. How much does each candidate pre-processing step — secondary hammer mill, conditioning mixer, or both — reduce feedstock variability and improve press stability under realistic supplier mixes?
3. Can a low-cost incoming inspection protocol combining NIR moisture checks with a simple particle-size screening method predict whether a delivery will cause a breakdown, and with what accuracy?

### Plan
| Year | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Co-define failure modes and collect baseline data from intake, pre-processing, and pellet-press downtime; instrument a trial campaign on representative sunflower husk lots from the 14 supplier contracts; map moisture, particle size, and press response. | A quantified acceptance envelope for incoming feedstock and a baseline model linking feedstock variance to breakdown risk. |
| 2 | Run controlled trials of secondary hammer milling and conditioning-mixer scenarios on out-of-spec deliveries; compare energy use, throughput, pellet quality, and press stability; develop a practical inspection workflow for intake staff. | A validated pre-processing specification showing which equipment combination reduces failures and by how much, plus a draft intake decision rule. |
| 3 | Validate the decision rule over a full operating season; refine a predictive maintenance and inspection protocol for the press; document implementation costs and savings against the current one-breakdown-per-month baseline. | An industrially tested feedstock-control package that reduces downtime risk and supports adoption at Suncokret Eko’s line. |

### What each side brings
- **Company:** Suncokret Eko brings access to the operating pellet line, monthly breakdown history, supplier variability across fourteen farm contracts, process logs, the in-line NIR sensor, two trained staff for trials, and the practical constraints of a real production schedule with 15,000 EUR lost per breakdown.
- **Faculty:** Faculty of Technical Sciences brings research supervision, expertise in reliability engineering and biomass densification, experimental design, stochastic degradation modelling, and condition-based maintenance methods for pellet mills. Supervisor: R0135 Veljko Savić, Associate Professor, Department of Production Engineering. The company-side supervisor should be Dragan Ostojić, process lead, with procurement input from Milica Vranić for supplier sampling and intake logistics.

### Why this pair
This pair matches a precise industrial reliability problem with the researcher’s core expertise: R0135 has documented experience in pellet mill reliability, feedstock variability, wear and degradation, vibration-based diagnostics, and condition-based maintenance for biomass densification equipment. Suncokret Eko has exactly the kind of operating line, supplier variation, and breakdown costs needed to turn that expertise into a usable acceptance standard and pre-processing specification. The collaboration is especially strong because the company already tracks feedstock and process data and can run controlled trials, while the researcher has prior industrial work on biomass densification reliability and on-site instrumentation in a related company setting.

### First step
Within the next month, hold a kickoff meeting to agree the sampling plan for 20 to 30 incoming loads, define the failure log fields to be standardised, and select the first trial matrix comparing current practice with one hammer-mill setting and one conditioning-mixer setting on out-of-spec material.

## Site-specific unsupervised anomaly detection for Modbus and BACnet traffic on ArmaturaSec ProtoSense appliances

**Pair:** Krisztina Kiss (R1008, Department of Computing and Control Engineering) with ArmaturaSec d.o.o. (C001), challenge C001-CH1
**Critic:** approved after 3 draft(s). Screening score 2.97 / 3.

### The problem
ArmaturaSec d.o.o. needs ProtoSense to detect unusual behaviour on Modbus and BACnet networks without any labelled attack data. The company has several terabytes of normal traffic captures from consenting clients, but no confirmed malicious traffic, and a previous supervised classifier trained on public ICS datasets produced too many false positives in real deployments, causing users to switch alerts off. The target solution must learn what normal looks like for a specific site, raise an alert when traffic drifts from that baseline, run on-device in the constrained embedded environment of ProtoSense, and be explainable to plant managers in plain language.

### Research questions
1. Can a site-specific baseline model built only from normal Modbus and BACnet traffic reduce false positives enough to keep alerts actionable in real deployments?
2. Which combination of statistical baseline modelling and one-class / unsupervised methods gives the best detection of behavioural drift while remaining lightweight enough for ProtoSense hardware?
3. What explanation format for anomaly alerts best helps plant managers interpret why a Modbus or BACnet sequence is flagged, without access to attack labels?

### Plan
| Year | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Collect and clean site-specific traffic from ArmaturaSec’s archives; define baseline features for Modbus and BACnet sequences; build and compare lightweight unsupervised and one-class models on the company’s archived normal traffic and on the university ICS testbed. | A validated baseline-detection prototype with initial thresholds, feature set, and an internal benchmark of false-positive rates on normal traffic. |
| 2 | Integrate the best model into ProtoSense firmware or its companion analysis pipeline; test inference cost, memory use, and latency on ArmaturaSec hardware; design explanation rules that summarize drift in protocol terms understandable to operators. | An embedded-ready anomaly detector that meets device constraints and produces operator-facing explanations for alerts. |
| 3 | Run a pilot at one or two consenting client sites with ArmaturaSec; tune the model for site adaptation and alert stability; evaluate operational usefulness with support engineers and plant staff. | A field-validated anomaly detection method and deployment guide for ArmaturaSec’s product line, ready for productization. |

### What each side brings
- **Company:** ArmaturaSec brings several terabytes of normal Modbus and BACnet traffic, access to ProtoSense hardware and firmware under NDA, its hardware bench with PLC simulators, and one engineer for part-time collaboration. Head of engineering Radovan Čičić can supervise the engineering side and provide product constraints from current deployments.
- **Faculty:** R1008 brings expertise in industrial cybersecurity, unsupervised anomaly detection, one-class classification, network traffic analysis, and statistical baseline modelling for ICS protocol traffic. Krisztina Kiss can supervise the research side, supported by her expertise record in industrial control system anomaly detection, network traffic analysis and protocol profiling, machine learning for unsupervised and one-class classification, statistical modelling and calibration for ICS monitoring, and federated/distributed ICS security monitoring.

### Why this pair
This pair matches the problem unusually well: ArmaturaSec’s challenge is specifically unsupervised detection on Modbus and BACnet traffic under embedded constraints, and R1008’s research record is centered on industrial control system anomaly detection, protocol traffic profiling, and lightweight unsupervised methods for operational environments. ArmaturaSec has the normal traffic and deployment context; R1008 has the methods and research expertise needed to build and validate a model without attack labels.

### First step
Next month, the two sides should hold a working session to define the first training corpus and evaluation protocol: select a representative slice of ProtoSense traffic archives, agree on which Modbus/BACnet features are allowed on-device, and set a baseline metric target for false alarms on normal traffic before any model is trained.
